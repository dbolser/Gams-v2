Flight handbook

Every body pulls on every other body. To orbit, a body needs sideways speed: too little and it falls in; enough extra speed and it can escape.

Add body: click for an automatic orbit, or untick the helper and drag an arrow in the direction you want to launch. A longer arrow means a faster launch. Manual launch speed is relative to the chosen centre.

Navigate: drag empty space to pan, scroll to zoom, click a body to select. Fit shows the whole system. Space plays / pauses; Escape switches to selection. On a keyboard, choose a body button to select it, and use + / − to zoom.

Experiment: Speed up fires along the direction of travel; Slow down fires the other way. They work on any selected body. Undo restores the moment before your last edit. Restart returns to the loaded system. Save captures the current moment.

A 2D Newtonian gravity toy, in made-up units (G = 40). Bodies merge on contact, conserving mass and momentum. Sizes are exaggerated; dashed paths and orbit numbers are two-body estimates, so moons and other stars can change them. Close encounters may slow time to keep the calculation stable. No atmosphere or fuel limits.
